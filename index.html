Phase Five · ~2–3 years Master's + ~2 years post-grad supervised hours
Master's Program + Full Licensure Future
Where you become what you've been building toward. Hardest phase. Most transformative.
▶
📖
Year 1 — Core Coursework
Trauma-informed care theory, DSM-5-TR assessment and diagnosis, ethics, individual and group therapy modalities, developmental theory, multicultural counseling, career development. Heavy reading and writing workload. Register for disability accommodations at grad school immediately — ADHD accommodations apply at this level too and are even more critical here.
🏥
Year 1–2 — Supervised Practicum (300–700 Hours)
Real clients, real clinical site, under licensed supervision. Request adolescent and young adult placements explicitly — this is your specialty track. This is the most impactful part of your entire education. Your lived experience becomes professional fuel here in a way that no amount of coursework can replicate.
// Trauma-focused modalities to pursue training hours in: TF-CBT (Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy), DBT for adolescents, EMDR. These are certifiable and highly valued in the field.
🏥
Year 2–3 — Internship (700–1,200+ Hours)
Extended clinical placement, often a different site. This is where you develop a clinical identity and specialize. Specifically seek placements with adolescents in trauma-informed settings — schools, residential treatment, outpatient mental health clinics. Each placement site is also a potential employer and a future recommendation source.
📜
Pass the NCE or NCMHCE Licensure Exam
National Counselor Examination (NCE) or National Clinical Mental Health Counseling Examination (NCMHCE) — Utah accepts both pathways. Pass rate is high for prepared candidates. Study consistently throughout the program, not just in the final weeks. You'll receive your Associate CMHC license upon passing, which allows supervised practice.
⏳
Post-Grad Supervised Hours → Full LCMHC License
Utah requires 3,000–4,000 supervised clinical hours post-graduation under a licensed supervisor before you can practice independently. This takes roughly 2 years of full-time work. During this period you're employed as an Associate CMHC — paid work, typically $40,000–$55,000/yr. This is not unpaid training. At completion, you receive your full independent LCMHC license.
// NHSC Loan Repayment is most applicable here — commit to working in an underserved area during these supervised hours and get significant loan forgiveness simultaneously.
⚠ Risk Zones
Secondary / vicarious trauma from working with traumatized teens — your own ongoing therapy during this phase is professional hygiene, not optional. This is standard practice for therapists.
Caretaking patterns bleeding into clinical work — the boundary between empathy and enmeshment is the specific thing to watch. Your therapist is your check.
Financial strain during practicum years — plan this 2 years ahead. Grad assistantship + NHSC is the target combo.
Burnout from high coursework + clinical hours simultaneously — build recovery time into your weekly schedule deliberately.
✓ Phase 5 Exit Conditions
MS in Clinical Mental Health Counseling in hand.
NCE or NCMHCE passed. Full LCMHC license issued.
3,000–4,000 supervised post-grad hours complete.
Independent clinical practice. You are now a licensed therapist.
Total timeline from today to this point: approximately 7–10 years.

Phase Six · ~20–30 years into independent practice · Optional but intentional
PhD in Counseling Psychology — Research + Legacy Long Game
You pursue this when you've hit a ceiling in what's known in the field. Not before. The clinical depth is what makes the research meaningful.
▶
// Why This Order Is The Right Order

Most PhD candidates in psychology enter research with 2–3 years of supervised experience at most. You'll enter with 20–30 years of direct clinical practice with the exact population you're researching. Your research questions won't come from gaps in literature — they'll come from thousands of hours in the room with real adolescents. That produces fundamentally better science. The clinical foundation isn't a detour. It's the asset.

// Prerequisites — What You Need to Have Before Applying
📋
20+ Years of Independent Clinical Practice
This isn't an arbitrary number — it's the threshold at which you'll have accumulated enough pattern recognition across enough clients to generate original research questions that practicing clinicians actually care about. You'll know when you're hitting the ceiling. That internal signal ("I keep seeing something the literature doesn't explain") is the trigger to start Phase 6 planning.
📝
Active Research Interest — Identify Your Question
PhD programs don't admit people who want to "do research generally." They admit people with specific, well-formed research interests. By the time you apply, you should be able to articulate: what specific phenomenon in trauma-informed adolescent mental health do you want to study, why it matters, and what methodology you'd use. Your decades of practice will generate this naturally — start paying attention to patterns early in your career and write them down.
// Keep a running clinical observations journal throughout your practice years. The patterns you notice repeatedly are your future research questions.
📖
Maintain Scholarly Engagement Throughout Practice
Read peer-reviewed journals throughout your career — Journal of Traumatic Stress, Journal of Counseling Psychology, Adolescent Psychiatry. Attend conferences (ACES, ACA annual conference). Present at local or regional workshops. Write articles for professional publications. This keeps you intellectually current and builds the scholarly identity PhD programs look for. It also means you won't be starting from zero when you apply.
🎤
Build Your Public Speaking Platform Before the PhD
Don't wait for the PhD to start speaking. A licensed LCMHC with 10–20 years of trauma-informed adolescent practice is already a credible expert voice. Start small — local workshops, school district trainings, community mental health presentations. Build to conference presentations and keynotes. By the time you apply to a PhD program, you'll already have a speaking portfolio. The PhD then amplifies that platform rather than creating it.
// The most compelling PhD applicants aren't just smart — they're already doing something in the field. You'll be one of those.
// The PhD Program Itself
🏛️
Target Program: University of Utah PhD in Counseling Psychology
APA-accredited — the highest standard in the field, required for psychology faculty positions and many research roles. Located 30 minutes from Pleasant Grove. Strong research infrastructure. By the time you apply, U of U faculty will know your name from the field — you won't be a stranger applying cold. This is the program where your clinical depth becomes a research superpower.
// APA accreditation matters: it's required if you ever want to supervise interns, hold a faculty position, or work in certain institutional research roles.
📋
PhD Application Requirements (What to Expect)
Master's degree ✅ (you'll have it). Licensure as LCMHC ✅. GRE — check current requirements, many programs have dropped this. Three letters of recommendation from professional and academic sources. Research statement (specific question + methodology + significance). Writing sample demonstrating scholarly ability. CV showing 20+ years of clinical work, presentations, publications, and professional service.
// At this stage your application will be unusual in the best way — most applicants are in their late 20s with minimal experience. You'll be in your 50s with a career that proves the work.
🔬
PhD Program Structure (4–6 Years)
Year 1–2: Advanced coursework — research methods, statistics, advanced theory, professional ethics at the doctoral level. Year 2–3: Comprehensive exams (written + oral) covering your specialty area. This is the academic gauntlet — pass these and you're a doctoral candidate. Year 3–4: Dissertation proposal — your original research question, literature review, and methodology get formally approved by your committee. Year 4–6: Data collection, analysis, writing, and dissertation defense. You are now Dr. Woodbury.
📊
Research Focus: Trauma-Informed Adolescent Mental Health
Your dissertation and research agenda will likely center on the intersection of trauma, adolescent development, and therapeutic outcomes — because that's where you'll have spent 20+ years developing original clinical insight. Possible research directions: efficacy of specific trauma modalities with different adolescent presentations, the role of therapist lived experience in treatment outcomes, systemic barriers to mental health access for at-risk youth, or longitudinal outcomes of trauma-informed school-based interventions.
// You won't know your exact research question until you've been practicing for years. That's correct. Leave this variable open and trust that your clinical work will generate it.
🎤
Post-PhD: Research + Speaking + Writing Platform
With a PhD + 25+ years of practice + LCMHC licensure, you occupy an extremely rare position: clinician-researcher with deep real-world credibility. This opens: academic faculty positions (teaching the next generation of therapists), keynote speaking at national conferences, book publishing (clinical nonfiction, practitioner guides, potentially public-facing books), consulting to school districts and healthcare systems, media appearances as a subject matter expert, and ongoing original research publication.
// The books you mentioned wanting to write? This is exactly the platform from which they get written — and read.
⚠ Risk Zones in Phase 6
Applying too early — a PhD pursued before you've hit the clinical ceiling produces generic research. The 20+ year wait is a feature, not a delay.
Losing scholarly engagement during practice years — if you go dark on journals and conferences for 15 years, re-entering academic culture is harder. Stay connected at a low level consistently.
Dissertation topic that's too broad — PhD committees reject proposals that try to answer everything. One specific, well-scoped research question is the target.
Underestimating the time commitment — a PhD while maintaining any clinical practice is a significant load. Plan financially and logistically for reduced practice hours during the dissertation years.
✓ Phase 6 Exit Conditions
PhD in Counseling Psychology (APA-accredited). Dissertation defended and published.
Active research agenda with at least one published peer-reviewed study.
Established speaking platform — regional or national conference presence.
Book project in progress or complete.
Total timeline from today: approximately 35–45 years. You'll be in your late 50s to mid 60s. That's not old — that's the peak of a life's work.
